# OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

This repository contains the core project management guidance used by OctoAcme to run cross-functional work from idea to delivery. The process begins with project initiation, where the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, creates a one-pager, and decides whether to advance into planning. Once the initiative is approved, the team moves into planning to define the backlog, milestone timeline, dependencies, and definition of done. This ensures work is scoped, prioritized, and ready for execution with clear ownership and measurable outcomes.

During execution, OctoAcme emphasizes iterative delivery, transparent communication, and visible progress tracking. Daily standups focus on progress, blockers, and dependencies; weekly delivery syncs provide a broader check-in on updates and risks; and demos or reviews help stakeholders see early outcomes and give feedback. The team uses project boards and backlog items with acceptance criteria, while pull requests are expected to be small, well-documented, and reviewed before merge. Quality is enforced through CI checks, tests, security scans, and manual QA where appropriate.

OctoAcme’s operating model relies on clear roles and responsibilities. Product managers define outcomes and priorities, project managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication, developers implement and validate the work, QA validates acceptance and quality, and stakeholders provide business context and approvals. These roles help keep delivery aligned with customer value while reducing uncertainty and making trade-offs visible. The process also supports a culture of continuous improvement by capturing lessons learned in retrospectives and converting them into actionable follow-ups.

Communication and risk management are built into the lifecycle. The team maintains a risk register, escalates dependencies and blockers through defined paths, and uses a single source of truth for status updates. Release and deployment work includes staging checks, smoke tests, rollback planning, and stakeholder communication. The guides in this folder provide a practical framework for initiating projects, planning execution, tracking progress, managing risks, and closing work with learning and improvement.
